EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
ShieldWealth AI is a portfolio suitability and compliance review system built with
Google's Agent Development Kit (ADK). Nine agents, run in sequence, profile a client,
read the market, audit the holdings against six suitability rules, propose a rebalance
that is re-audited before it is recommended, find matching funds with FAISS retrieval,
and write a plain-language verdict with a downloadable PDF report. Rule thresholds are
applied against each client's own age, goal, time horizon, account mix, debt and stated
risk tolerance rather than one fixed bucket. Every number comes from a deterministic
rules engine, and the whole pipeline can run offline with no API key. All client data
is synthetic.


1. THE "WHY" — THE PROBLEM & MARKET OPPORTUNITY
-----------------------------------------------

### The Advisory Bottleneck
Traditionally, financial firms segment clients into broad, static buckets based on
arbitrary brackets of age or income. Fiduciary advice is standardized because
providing deep, dynamic, client-specific analysis is highly labor-intensive and
operationally expensive. Consequently:
- Mass Retail Clients receive generic, "one-size-fits-all" asset allocations that
  ignore major personal nuances (such as a short-term home purchase goal or unique
  cash-flow timelines).
- Affluent Clients receive premium, bespoke advisory services, creating a massive
  wealth advice gap.
- Compliance Teams are burdened with checking static check-boxes (e.g.,
  "alternative assets must not exceed 10%"), failing to analyze if the allocation
  is truly suitable for the client's concrete life path.

### The Opportunity
As client expectations shift toward dynamic cost-efficiency and personalized wealth
strategies, firms need an automated system that can analyze a client's full financial
picture, audit risk flags dynamically, and write actionable rebalancing strategies—
all while keeping a clear, reviewable audit trail.


2. THE "WHAT" — OUR SOLUTION: PERSONALIZED RULES
------------------------------------------------
Instead of applying one flat checklist to everyone, ShieldWealth AI decides which
rules apply to a client, and what each limit is, from that client's own profile.
The thresholds live in data/suitability_rules.json; the checks are implemented in
evaluate_compliance in app/engine.py.

### The Six Suitability Rules
* R1 · Retirement money vs. short goal: Flags a client whose goal is 3 years or less
  away, who holds money in a 401(k) or IRA, who will still be under 59½ at the goal
  date, and whose money reachable without penalty (savings, checking, taxable
  brokerage) is less than the goal amount. Such a client may have to withdraw early
  and pay penalties. A client who will be 59½ or older by the goal date passes.
* R2 · Single-position concentration: Flags any one stock or narrow fund above 30%
  of the portfolio. Cash and broadly diversified index funds are exempt.
* R3 · Volatility vs. conservative profile: Flags a conservative client holding more
  than 15% in high-volatility holdings.
* R4 · Liquidity need vs. accessible money: Flags a client with a high liquidity need
  who has less than 25% in savings, checking or taxable brokerage accounts.
* R5 · Debt-adjusted risk: Flags a client with more than $20,000 of non-mortgage debt
  who holds more than 20% in high-volatility assets.
* R6 · Age-based equity ceiling: For clients aged 55 or older, flags equity above the
  age-bracket benchmark plus 15 points. Benchmarks come from
  data/age_allocation_norms.json (roughly 110 minus age, e.g. 55% equity for ages
  50–59).

The health score is 100 minus 15 per breached rule. Priority is High for two or more
breaches or any breach of R3, R5 or R6, Medium for a single other breach, and Low
when the portfolio is compliant.

### Advisory Checks Alongside the Rules
Separate from the hard rules, an advisory risk check (assess_risk) compares the
portfolio with the client's stated risk tolerance: a conservative profile allows 0%
high-volatility holdings and a moderate one 30%, with equity bands for each profile.
Findings are raised in severity when the VIX is elevated. A financial-stability score
deducts points for variable income, self-employment, high debt-to-income and weaker
credit. These checks inform the strategy and summary but do not change the
compliance verdict or health score.


3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & AGENT DESIGN
-------------------------------------
The pipeline is an ADK SequentialAgent (wealth_suitability_pipeline, built by
build_pipeline in app/agent.py) with nine agents. Agents 4–6 run inside a nested
SequentialAgent, portfolio_review_stage.

### Decoupled State Coordination
Agents never call each other. Each one calls its tools, which read from and write
to typed ADK session-state keys:
1. Intake Agent: get_client → save_client_profile → 'client_profile'
2. Market Scout Agent: fetch_market_context (Baseline, Volatility shock, Inverted
   curve, or Live from Yahoo Finance) → 'market_context'
3. Personal Financial Analyst Agent: analyze_financial_stability →
   'financial_stability_profile'
4. Portfolio Analysis Agent: fetch_and_calculate_portfolio (allocations vs. age
   benchmark) → 'portfolio_metrics', 'holdings'
5. Risk Assessment Agent: assess_portfolio_risk → 'risk_flags'
6. Compliance Agent: evaluate_compliance_rules (R1–R6) → 'compliance_result'
   (PASS or FLAG/REJECT)
7. Planning & Strategy Agent: get_strategy_baseline → save_planning_strategy →
   'rebalance_plan', 'planning_strategy'
8. Product Research Agent: query_product_research (FAISS over a 24-fund prospectus
   corpus, filtered by the client's risk profile) → 'product_research_result'
9. Advisor Summary Agent: draft_advisor_summary → save_final_summary →
   'final_summary'

### The Numbers Come From Code, Not the Model
Every calculation (allocations, rule verdicts, health score, priority, trades) lives
in app/engine.py. The language model decides which tools to call and writes the
client-facing wording. In Offline mode, a deterministic stand-in model (OfflineLlm in
app/offline_llm.py) drives the same tools in order and writes nothing of its own; in
Gemini mode the agents run on gemini-2.5-flash.

### MCP-Compatible Data Functions
mcp_server.py defines the data access functions (get_client, get_holdings,
get_suitability_rules, get_age_allocation_norms, get_ticker_reference) as FastMCP
tools. Client lookups are scoped to one validated client ID ("C" followed by digits)
to block bulk access and malformed IDs. In this build the engine imports these
functions as plain Python rather than calling them over the MCP protocol, and the
client-book view loads clients.csv directly, so the server can be run standalone but
is not yet a hard isolation boundary.

### Re-Audited Rebalancing
A breach doesn't just return "Fail". The planning step calls propose_rebalance, which
trims the positions behind each breach that trading can fix (R2, R3, R5, R6) and
reinvests the proceeds inside the same account, so no withdrawals are implied.
Proceeds go to a bond fund (BND or AGG) and a money-market fund (VMFXX) in the
age-benchmark mix, to VMFXX alone for short-horizon or high-liquidity clients, or to
VXUS when only a concentration trim was needed and equity is below benchmark. After
each pass it re-runs the full R1–R6 audit on the proposed portfolio and repeats, up
to four passes, until no fixable rule is breached. The plan reports the before and
after audits and health scores, so it states whether it actually clears the rules
rather than assuming so. R1 and R4 depend on which account the money is in, so they
come back as advisor actions instead of trades. This loop is ordinary Python inside
the engine, not a separate agent loop.

### Guardrails on the Model
* Client profile: save_client_profile always saves the record get_client fetched.
  If the model's JSON differs, the differing fields are logged and ignored.
* Final summary: save_final_summary rebuilds the engine's draft and calls
  reconcile_summary. It always restores the computed fields (health score, priority,
  status, estimated annual income, checked items). It rejects rewritten wording, and
  keeps the engine's text, when the wording states a number, ticker or rule ID the
  draft doesn't contain, claims approval for a client who isn't compliant, or drops
  or adds findings. The UI shows each correction it makes.


4. THE PROJECT JOURNEY & DESIGN ITERATIONS
------------------------------------------

### Phase 1: Static Audits & Broad Categories
We began by building a simple pipeline where compliance thresholds were fixed. While
it successfully automated rules verification, it suffered from the same rigidity as
traditional banking systems—failing to adjust limits for younger, long-term investors
vs. older, short-term savers.

### Phase 2: Client-Aware Rules Engine
We moved every calculation into one deterministic engine and made each rule depend
on the client's own attributes: risk tolerance (R3), debt (R5), age benchmark (R6),
liquidity need (R4), and goal amount, horizon and age at the goal date (R1). A
conservative client buying a home soon is audited under different conditions than a
moderate client planning retirement.

### Phase 3: Re-Audited Rebalancing
Initially, a rule breach simply returned a "Fail" status. We added a rebalancer that
proposes exact trades (e.g., sell part of a concentrated position and buy BND in the
same account), re-audits the result, and repeats until the fixable breaches clear,
handing the account-dependent rules to the advisor.

### Phase 4: Guardrails, Testing & Packaging
We added the profile and summary guardrails so the model cannot change client data
or invent facts in the verdict. We built a light compliance-workbench UI in
Streamlit, a PDF exporter that maps symbols to plain text so generation never fails
on unsupported characters, and an offline mode that runs all nine agents with no API
key. The test suite runs offline and covers rule boundaries, the rebalancer across
every demo client, the guardrails, and all 40 clients through the full ADK pipeline.


5. TRUST, FIDUCIARY RESPONSIBILITY, & TRANSPARENCY
--------------------------------------------------
For AI to be deployed in financial services, it must be explainable and keep people
in control:
* Rule-Driven Product Choices: Rebalancing trades use a fixed, low-cost set of broad
  funds (BND, AGG, VMFXX, VXUS) chosen by rule, and fund research ranks prospectuses
  by how well they match the strategy, excluding funds more volatile than the client's
  risk profile allows. The system names specific funds; it does not favour any
  in-house products.
* Explainable Reports: Every review produces a PDF with the rule-by-rule audit
  (threshold, actual value and result), breach details, allocation vs. age benchmark,
  the proposed trades and advisor actions, fund research, market context and the
  agent audit trail.
* Data Privacy: In Offline mode the deterministic model runs locally, so client
  data never leaves the machine. In Gemini mode, prompts that include client data
  are sent to Google's API. The Live market scenario fetches only market data
  (VIX and Treasury yields) from Yahoo Finance.
* Limits: Client data, prices and the fund corpus are synthetic or fixed snapshots.
  Rebalancing ignores tax lots, transaction costs and wash-sale rules. This is a
  demonstration, not investment advice.
